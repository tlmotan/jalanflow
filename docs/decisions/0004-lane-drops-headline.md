# 0004 — Site headline is lane drops on the route

- Status: accepted · 2026-11-23 (owner chose it 2026-11-22)
- Context: With one gate per side, the whole-network min cut is KESAS itself at the Kota Kemuning gate (0003), which says little. The two places the owner jams every day — the Sri Muda entry onto KESAS eastbound and the exit-510 ramp into Subang — are not min cuts. Both are **lane drops**: more lanes come in than carry on (2 + 3 → 4, and 3 → 2).
- Decision: The site headline is a ranked list of lane drops on KESAS (E5) + LDP (E11) and the ramps touching them (`internal/lanedrop`, ARCHITECTURE §4.3b). Ranked **by size**: squeeze = lanes out ÷ lanes in, smallest first; ties go to more lanes lost. Spots a local has checked on Street View are marked "confirmed" via `lane_drops.confirmed` in the corridor config, keyed by OSM node or way pair. Max flow / fixes stay in Phase 1 as secondary views.
- Filters (prototyped on 2026-11-22 OSM; 2,233 raw drops → 88): grade-separated classes only (motorway/trunk and links); skip signalised junctions (turn-taking makes in > out normal); skip points within 400 m of a `barrier=toll_booth` (plazas widen to 11–15 lanes and narrow back).
- Consequences: The ranking counts lanes, not traffic, so it cannot say which drop jams worst. Sri Muda (5 → 4) ranks #69 of 88 and Subang (3 → 2) #31, even though both are known jams; the site must say why. Ranks resting on guessed lane counts (`lanes_inferred`) are flagged; the current top 2 are such. Not route choice: no paths or driver behaviour are modelled, only lane counts at points on named roads.
