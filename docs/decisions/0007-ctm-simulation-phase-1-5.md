# 0007 — Phase 1.5 simulates the corridor with our own Cell Transmission Model

- Status: accepted · 2026-11-23
- Context: Phase 1 counts lanes and capacity but has no traffic demand, so it cannot say how bad a jam is, where the queue reaches, or whether a fix saves anyone time. The two known jams (Sri Muda queue from lane drop #21 at the exit-507 merge; exit-510 Subang ramp) are not min cuts (0004). The owner approved Phase 1.5: add demand and a simulator so the site can recommend **tested** fixes ranked by delay saved. Phase 1 stays as shipped; the site stays static (0001).
- Decision: Own **Daganzo Cell Transmission Model** in a new pure package `internal/ctm` (no I/O, no logging, no internal imports, like `flow`). The corridor is a chain of cells (~200–500 m) per direction on KESAS E5 + LDP E11, with on-ramp source cells and off-ramp sink cells. Fundamental diagram per cell: triangular (free-flow speed, capacity, jam density, backward wave speed), from lanes and `config/capacity-defaults.json`; any new number goes in config with a `source`. Extensions: **capacity drop** at active bottlenecks (discharge falls once a queue forms), **ALINEA** ramp metering, and **stepped signals** (cell capacity 0 on red). A second new package `internal/corridor` builds the cell chain from the graph (`corridor` ← `graph`, `config`, `ctm`). Output is precomputed by `cmd/analyze` into `scenarios.json`.
- Why CTM, not SUMO or another microsimulator: SUMO is C++ with its own network format and toolchain (breaks "no new dependencies" and the Go-only pipeline); microsimulation needs driver-behaviour parameters and lane-level geometry we cannot calibrate from public data; results are stochastic and need many seeds. CTM is ~300 lines, deterministic, fast (a peak in milliseconds, so every fix can be re-run), reproduces queue formation and spillback, and its few parameters map to things we have (lanes, capacity, speed). It is also a good interview topic, like Dinic (0002).
- **Fixed-route assumption:** demand enters at on-ramps and leaves at off-ramps by measured split ratios; nobody changes route because of a jam or a fix. This keeps clear of route choice (PRD non-goal, Phase 3). Consequence: induced demand and diversion are not modelled, so savings are "with today's traffic". The site says so next to every result.
- Data sources: mainline and ramp volumes from **JKR/HPU Road Traffic Volume Malaysia** (census stations, AADT + hourly profiles) and **LLM** (Lembaga Lebuhraya Malaysia) highway statistics (plaza transactions); peak factors and split ratios from those, filled with stated assumptions where missing. Calibration targets: **Google Maps typical travel times** for the peak (sections of the commute, read manually and recorded with date and time), and **local driver observations** of where queues start and end (owner, Street View checks). Every input carries a source and year in config.
- **Validation gate:** the baseline must (1) reproduce the Sri Muda queue with its head at drop #21 (exit-507 merge), (2) reproduce the exit-510 Subang ramp queue, and (3) match peak travel times on the calibrated sections within **≤ 20 %** error. Until all three pass, `scenarios.json` publishes no fixes (only the baseline, marked "not validated"). Results go in `docs/validation.md`.
- Consequences: new config for demand, splits and signal timings (all with `source`); `internal/ctm` must have table tests (conservation of vehicles, queue at a known bottleneck, capacity-drop discharge, ALINEA convergence). The CTM cannot see geometry finer than a cell (see 0008 for what that means for dividers). Junction capacity off the chain is still unmodelled.
